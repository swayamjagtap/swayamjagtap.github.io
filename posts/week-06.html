The Muddy Trousers, A Fictional Crisis, and The VibeHack Aftermath

September 8, 2026

“OHHH MY LORD SWAYAMMMM!!! You do remember that you are supposed to write blogs every Saturday?!!!”

That is probably exactly what popped into your head when you saw this Week 6 blog rolling out more than two weeks late. 😅😅

But let me tell you something. The past two weeks were a wild mix of absolute awesomeness and pure frustration! The highlight? VibeHack’26. It was my department's (and probably the entire college's) first-ever hackathon! If you've been following my blogs, you already know I was THE CHOSEN ONE 🗿 to see this event through.

Dayyymmm, what a day it was.

It didn’t start out very nice, though. Those pristine white bottoms I specifically saved for the main event? Yeah, completely covered in mud. Why? Because I spent HALF AN HOUR standing in the pouring rain at Gundavli trying to hire an auto, carrying my backpack and three other bags weighing over 20kgs altogether!

But koina, a rocky start couldn't ruin the day. I’d be lying if I said the rest of the event went "smoothly"—for those 4 to 5 hours, I was literally sprinting between the two labs assigned to us. As the organizer, I had to make sure the participants didn't face a single bottleneck throughout the event. This is also exactly why I ended up with zero photos or videos of myself from that day. It’s like I wasn't even there! 🫠❤️‍🩹

Nevertheless, I am beyond glad I put in the sweat (and the mud) because the participants absolutely LOVED it.

Let's talk about the Problem Statement for a second. I could have handed everyone a boring, bullet-pointed PDF with a generic task. But honestly, if you're running the show, you have to give the people a show (Max Verstappen "Are you not entertained?!" meme)

Instead of a standard lab assignment, my absolutely goated brain decided it was a good idea to forge a literal piece of art—an entire newspaper (shoutout to The MLDC Times) detailing the digital disruption of a fictional e-commerce company called "Clinkt." It had cart abandonments, inventory puzzles, and recommendation system failures. I wanted the participants to feel like real-world strategists reading the morning financial news, not just students solving a textbook problem.

Actually, thinking about it… I am going to open-source this entire challenge. Very soon, I’ll be dropping the exact newspaper PDF and the raw dataset right here on the website. Consider this an official heads-up: I will be challenging all of you to step into the arena yourselves and see if you can solve the Clinkt crisis.

But until then, you'll just have to take my word for how well it played out. Or better yet, take their word for it. Reading the feedback from both the tech and commerce students made all the mud, the sprinting, and the chaos 100% worth it. Here is just a glimpse of what they had to say:
