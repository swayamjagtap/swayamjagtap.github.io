Mid-Week Drop: So... We're Starting a Startup Now? Wait, Who's Paying for It? 😅

November 9, 2026

So, remember in the previous blog I told you that something was brewing in the background?

Well....... it was RAAH.

Aavishkar, RAAH and the sudden realisation that building technology isn't everything

We are representing our college in the University of Mumbai's Aavishkar competition, where we are working on an idea built on top of RESQNET, the MVP/prototype we previously developed for Elevate (the national-level hackathon we talked about earlier).

The idea had been evolving, and we'd been working on developing it further for the competition.

Our professors who were mentoring us genuinely liked the concept. In fact, they even suggested that we could potentially turn it into a startup!

And obviously, hearing something like that gets you excited😭😎

But then came a series of questions that none of us had really prepared ourselves for. They pointed out that while our technical idea had potential, we hadn't properly established its business side.

Questions like:

  • Why should someone invest in us?
  • What exactly are we bringing to the table?
  • What's the mutual benefit for the people involved?
  • How are we planning to generate revenue?
  • What's our business model?
  • Why would someone choose our solution over existing alternatives?

And suddenly, the entire room went quiet🫠

Because here's the thing: everyone in our team comes from a technical background. We were comfortable discussing the architecture, functionalities, workflows and implementation.

But business? Finance? Revenue models? Investor expectations?

Yeahhhh... those were practically alien territories for us😭

We could explain how our system worked, but explaining why someone should invest money into making it work was an entirely different challenge.

And that got us thinking.

From writing code to thinking about money 💸

We started researching.

Government schemes, startup policies, funding opportunities, possible support programmes... basically anything that could help us understand how an idea like RAAH could move beyond a college prototype.

Some of the opportunities we came across looked genuinely promising.

I also spoke to a few relatives working in corporate environments and explained the concept to them.

They seemed interested in the overall idea, but more importantly, they pointed out a few gaps that we needed to address.

And honestly, that was exactly what I wanted. Not someone telling me the idea was brilliant just to make me feel good. I wanted people to question it, challenge our assumptions and point out things we hadn't considered.

Because what's the point of asking for feedback if you're only interested in hearing compliments?!?!😭😭

During this period, I also happened to visit another relative.

Now, there's this particular guy in the family — my mom's cousin brother. He isn't exactly the most talkative person around, and I barely knew anything about his professional background. Which is slightly embarrassing considering he's family😅😭

Anyway, I finally gathered enough confidence to ask him what he actually did.

Turns out, he's been working at his company for around 6–7 years and currently holds a Director-level position in Sales and Marketing!!!

Well, that's interesting. So naturally, I brought up RESQNET.

I explained the idea, what we'd built, and how we were now developing RAAH on top of it. What started as a casual conversation turned into a discussion lasting around 20–25 minutes. He seemed to like the concept and then mentioned something that immediately caught my attention.

Apparently, the founder of his company is interested in investing in and funding startups.

And not just that.

He suggested that she might be able to help us understand and develop the business side of our idea through mentorship.

Things like:

  • Figuring out how the startup could generate revenue.
  • Understanding what investors actually look for.
  • Identifying what makes our solution different from existing alternatives.
  • Learning how to present and pitch the idea.
  • Understanding what value we could offer to potential partners and investors.

Now THAT was interesting.

Because we had spent so much time thinking about how to build the product that we'd barely explored how to turn it into a sustainable business.

And suddenly, there was a possibility of getting guidance from someone with actual experience in that space😁

Enter: sapne suhane investors edition 😏💸

At this point, you can probably guess what was happening inside my head.

We were already in a pretty good momentum of building things, exploring possibilities, speaking to people and figuring out where RAAH could go.

And now we had the possibility of someone helping us with the business side of things?

My brain immediately skipped approximately 47 steps!!

From discussing a college prototype to imagining a fully functioning startup, funding, investors and printing money like crazyyyyyyyyyyy!!

Sapne suhane ladakpan ke😭💸

I was genuinely excited. It felt like things were finally lining up.

We had an idea, a working prototype, professors who believed in its potential, feedback from people in corporate environments, and now possibly an opportunity to learn directly from someone involved in funding startups.

What could possibly go wrong?

Well...

Apparently, quite a lot🥲🫠

Turns out, I was looking at the startup world through some rather optimistic glasses😅🤧

And then came Devcon 8.

A few conversations with founders, investors and developers from different parts of the world gave my little startup daydream a reality check I wasn't quite prepared for😭

Let's just say I walked in with a head full of ideas and walked out with a LOT more questions.

But that's a rabbit hole of its own.

And trust me, that deserves an entire blog👀
