Anywayyyyy, I've been occupied with A LOT of stuff lately. Two hackathons, website upgrades, almost finishing my F1 Telemetry project, college competitions, research, and a whole bunch of other things that my dumb-yet-elite brain has conveniently decided not to remember.

So, where do I even begin?

Let's rewind a little.

The 31st-place heartbreak🗿

One of the major things that kept me occupied was participating in a national-level hackathon.

Now, this wasn't your typical build-something-and-go-home hackathon. The competition had two rounds.

The first round was conducted online, with approximately 375–400 teams participating from across India. And only the top 30 teams would qualify for the next round: a 24-hour offline hackathon.

And guess where we finished?

31st.

ONE. POSITION. AWAY.

😭😭😭

Now, don't get me wrong. We were genuinely happy with what we'd achieved. Ranking 31st among roughly 400 teams across the country wasn't exactly a small accomplishment.

But there was one tiny problem.

The Max Verstappen inside me has absolutely zero chill when it comes to competition.

We were THIS close to making it through, and I couldn't help thinking about what could've happened if we'd just squeezed into that top 30.

I was disappointed in myself.

But then I looked at my teammates. They were dancing, celebrating, and telling practically everyone around them that we'd secured 31st place out of 375+ teams.

And honestly, seeing them that happy made me realise something…… We had worked hard for this. We had made it through an extremely competitive online round, and we'd accomplished something worth celebrating.

I wasn't going to be the guy standing in the corner mourning the one position we missed while everyone else was enjoying the moment😅😅

So I took a step back, stopped behaving like I'd just lost a championship by 0.001 seconds, and enjoyed the achievement with them😭❤️

Still...

P31.

I'll remember that one🗿

Apparently, I signed up for a cybersecurity hackathon too 💀

The other hackathon I participated in was an international, cybersecurity-oriented one. And here's the interesting part. Cybersecurity isn't exactly my area of expertise😅

But hey, that's never stopped me from wandering into unfamiliar technical territories before😎😏

So, what did I build???

A Privacy Policy Decoder.

We've all encountered those ‘ridiculously long privacy policies’ filled with legal jargon, complicated clauses and terms that make you question whether you accidentally opened a law textbook.

And let's be honest, how many of us actually read the entire thing before clicking I Agree?

Exactly!!!

The idea behind my project was simple: allow someone to copy and paste a privacy policy, and let AI break it down into simpler, more understandable language.

Instead of making an ordinary user navigate through paragraphs of legal-heavy-loopy terms, the tool would help them understand what they're actually agreeing to.

Because apparently, protecting your privacy shouldn't require a law degree🤷‍♂️🤷‍♂️

It was a nice opportunity to step outside the areas I usually work in and explore something different. And honestly, that was one of the things I enjoyed about participating.

I wasn't necessarily working with technologies I was already comfortable with, but I got to experiment with a problem I hadn't previously explored.

From organising hackathons to awarding the winners 😎

Speaking of hackathons, there was another little event on the calendar.

We eventually got around to awarding the VibeHack winners, along with the winners of the other events we hosted through DataVerse.

After spending so much time organising challenges, preparing material and managing the events, it was genuinely nice to see everything come together and recognise the participants.

It's one thing to participate in a competition. It's another to be on the organising side of things, help create the experience, and then watch people actually engage with what you've put together.

A completely different set of responsibilities, but a pretty satisfying experience nonetheless😁

A completely unrelated skateboard side quest 😅🗿

Now, somewhere between all this technical chaos, I also managed to unlock a completely unexpected skill😎

I was out with some of my juniors at a mall, and we ended up visiting Decathlon. And naturally, what does one do at Decathlon?

Try random things they have absolutely no business trying🗿

There was a skateboard. I had never skateboarded before. But I decided to give it a shot anyway. And somehow...

I WAS GLIDING😆!!!

Not falling every two seconds. Not holding onto someone for dear life. Actually gliding pretty decently!! The funniest part was that nobody was ready to believe me when I told them it was my first time😭😭

I still don't know whether I discovered a hidden talent or just got ridiculously lucky. But I'll take it. Adding professional skateboarder to my resume as we speak😎
